Chapter 01 · Prove

Two new merchants. Find their leaks.

These are merchants you didn't meet in the video — different verticals on purpose, so it tests skill, not recall. For each, name the dominant leak and two secondaries in the merchant's own words, then size at least one with the Pain Map math. Write your answer, then reveal the model answer to check yourself.

Scenario A · Timberline Building Supply — lumber yard

"We've been doing this 30 years and honestly the card fees just feel like the cost of doing business at this point — I eat them, I don't pass them to customers, but every month it's a bigger number and I don't totally understand what I'm even paying for on that statement. What really gets me is I've got two yards now and I can't tell what's happening at the second one until my office manager pulls it all together at month-end. And getting paid takes forever — I make a sale today and the money's not in my account for days. My accounting software and whatever the card thing is don't talk to each other, so my bookkeeper's basically retyping everything."

Model answer — Timberline

  • Dominant: high processing fees. "Getting killed on cards," doesn't understand the statement.
  • Secondaries: slow deposits ("money's not in my account for days"), no cross-location visibility ("can't tell what's happening at the second yard"), disconnected tools ("don't talk to each other").
  • Sizing: monthly fees ÷ monthly card volume → effective rate, then compare to an optimized rate.
Scenario B · Delta Asphalt & Aggregates — asphalt / aggregates supplier

"We got absolutely torched on a couple of disputes last quarter — material was already delivered and poured, gone, and we still ate the loss plus the fee. Our drivers are still collecting checks out on the route half the time, which is its own headache. And when we do take a card, sometimes it's over the phone and my guy just writes the number on the ticket. I know that's probably not great but that's how we've always done it. I couldn't tell you what any given transaction actually looks like — the statement's a mess."

Model answer — Delta

  • Dominant: chargeback losses. "Torched on disputes," material gone, ate the loss plus the fee.
  • Secondaries: drivers collecting in the field, PCI / compliance exposure ("writes the number on the ticket"), no transaction-level clarity ("statement's a mess").
  • Sizing: chargebacks/mo × (avg ticket + chargeback fee).
